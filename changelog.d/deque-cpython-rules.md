- Dynamic builds: `collections.deque` follows CPython's rules: iterators, `in`, `count`, `index` and `remove` raise when the deque changes under them; `+`, `*` and `*=` keep `maxlen`; `<`/`<=`/`>`/`>=` compare deques; `insert` into a full deque raises `IndexError`; `index` takes start and stop; `__init__`, `__getitem__`, `__copy__` and the other dunders exist; argument errors match CPython's.
- Dynamic builds: a deque holding itself prints `[...]` instead of overflowing the stack; a deque subclass prints its own class name; `deque` reports module `collections`; `id()` of a deque, set, iterator or function is its own address (it was 1 for all of them); deques take weak references.
- Typed builds: `insert` on a queue clamps an index past either end, as `list.insert` does.
- Dynamic builds: `pickle.dumps` and `pickle.dump` take deques, their iterators and instances of deque subclasses (with their `__dict__` and `__slots__` values), in CPython's own `deque.__reduce__` form, and `pickle.loads` gives them back as the program's own (a deque holding itself still holds itself). Any other library still refuses a deque, since it would get a copy. Pickling a subclass that defines its own `__reduce__`, `__getstate__`, `extend` or `append`, or an iterator over a subclass instance, stops with a clear "not supported" message.
- Dynamic builds: `subclass * n`, `n * subclass` and `subclass + d` of a deque subclass give an instance of the subclass, `maxlen` of a subclass instance works, and `str()` of one is its repr, as in CPython.
- Typed builds: `list.insert` in a `.py` file clamps an index past either end, as CPython does (it stopped with "index out of range").
- Conformance: CPython's `test_deque` joins the stdlib slice (97.2% matched, no wrong result; its one unmatched test uses `eval`).
